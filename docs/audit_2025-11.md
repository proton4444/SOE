# Repository Consolidation & Defect Audit — v0.7.1

This document records the November 2025 consolidation of the repository onto a
single engine, and the defects found and fixed in that engine.

## 1. Consolidation

The repository had drifted into two independent implementations that shared no
source files, plus a `main` branch that contained only `rules.md` and no code:

| Branch | Package | State |
|---|---|---|
| `codex/review-code-for-missing-rules` | `soe/` | Full PBEM turn engine, CLI, persistence, reports — v0.7.0 |
| `claude/check-rules-…` | `src/soe/` | Layered command prototype, 22 of 81 commands, no working persistence |
| `main` | — | `rules.md` only |

`main` was fast-forwarded to the `soe/` lineage, which is now the
single source of truth. The `src/soe/` prototype is retired; it remains
reachable on its original branch if anything needs recovering from it. Its one
genuinely better idea — a declarative command registry mapping order types to
handler classes — is worth revisiting if `engine.py`'s phase functions keep
growing.

## 2. Defects fixed

Ordered roughly by severity. Each has a regression test in
`tests/test_regressions.py` that fails against the pre-audit code.

### Save/load silently discarded most of the game

`storage.decode_game_state` rebuilt each model by naming fields explicitly and
had fallen behind the models. Every load dropped: character `gender`, `title`,
`is_prisoner`, `captor_id`, `religion_skill`, `religious_power_current`,
`trading_skill`, `is_dead` and `resources`; faction `secured_city_ids`,
`allies`, `enemies` and `fortifications`; city `fortification_level` and
`resource_richness`; and the entire `summoned_creatures`, `city_fortifications`,
`tax_pools`, `location_blessings` and `location_curses` collections.

In a PBEM game every turn is a save/load cycle, so prisoners walked free,
diplomacy reset, and religion and mining state vanished each turn. Serialization
is now driven by the dataclass field definitions, so a new field on a model is
persisted without a matching edit here. Writes are also atomic — the previous
implementation could leave a truncated `state.json` if interrupted.

### CAPTURE crashed turn processing

`parse_capture_order` passed `enemy_ok=True` to `resolve_character`, which had
no such parameter. Both of its target-resolution paths did so, meaning any
capture order raised `TypeError` and aborted the whole turn. The parameter now
exists and does what the call sites assumed.

(An earlier draft of this document said "CAPTURE and ENSLAVE". `ENSLAVE` is not
implemented at all — see the command reference in `MECHANICS.md`.)

### Players could act with other factions' characters

`resolve_character` searched the issuing player's faction first but then fell
back to *every* faction, so naming an opponent's character bound them as your
order's actor. `validate_orders` only checked ownership for 6 of ~30 order
types, so for the rest — `TAX`, `COLLECT`, `MINE`, `BUILD`, `TRADE`, `STUDY`,
`ASSIGN` and others — the order simply went through, crediting the proceeds to
the issuing faction.

Actor resolution is now confined to the player's own characters; `enemy_ok=True`
is opt-in and used only for genuine targets (attack, capture, freeing a
prisoner, gifts to another faction). All order types now pass through a single
`actor_can_act` gate.

### Dead characters and prisoners kept acting

`is_dead` was checked only in the phases added most recently; movement, combat,
tax, trade and the rest ignored it. `is_prisoner` gated nothing at all. Both are
now enforced centrally.

### City income was counted twice

`process_income_and_upkeep` added each city's income to the faction treasury
*and* to that city's tax pool, and `process_tax` then paid the pool out to the
treasury again — so every gold piece could be collected twice.

Income now accrues to the tax pool only and reaches the treasury through a `TAX`
order, matching both `rules.md` and the documented design. This is a visible
gameplay change: factions that never issue `TAX` no longer receive income, while
upkeep is still charged every turn.

### TAX ignored enemy-secured cities

The guard rejecting collection in a city secured by another faction used
`continue` inside the loop over factions, which advanced only that inner loop.
The order then proceeded to collect anyway.

### Characters marched across open water

`find_shortest_path` traversed the entire road graph including sea lanes, so a
character could walk from island to island without a ship. Land and sea are now
disjoint networks. The two near-identical Dijkstra implementations were merged
into one parameterised routine.

### Movement was effectively free on good roads

Movement cost was applied as `movement_points -= int(cost)`. An excellent road
costs 0.5, which truncates to 0, so a character could cross an arbitrarily long
chain of good roads in one turn. Cost is now rounded up, minimum 1 per hop.

### One galley could ferry an unlimited army

`process_sail` moved every friendly unit stack in the port to the destination
regardless of the ship's capacity. Units are now loaded up to capacity, with
stacks split at the waterline, and ships may only dock at ports.

### Gifted gold was destroyed

`ASSIGN`/`GIVE` deducted gold from the donor's treasury and never credited
anyone. Transferred units were also created in the *donor's* faction at the
recipient's location, so gifts to another faction quietly stayed with the giver.
Both now transfer properly.

Separately, the implicit form (`give 100 gold to Bill`, with no named donor) set
`donor_id` to a *faction* id rather than a character id, so the engine failed to
find the donor and skipped the order silently. It now resolves to the faction
leader.

### Entity IDs could collide and overwrite live entities

New stacks, ships, characters and creatures took ids of the form
`f"{prefix}_{len(registry) + 1}"`. After any removal — a stack wiped out in
combat, for instance — that formula returns an id already in use, and the
assignment overwrites a live entity. All allocation now goes through
`allocate_id`, which skips taken ids. Two id sites also drew from the seeded RNG
purely to generate a suffix, which made every later random draw in the turn
depend on how many units had been recruited; they no longer consume the RNG.

### Trade prices were taken from the order

`TradeOrder` carried a `price` field that `process_trade` trusted
(`order.price or 5`). No parser populated it, so this was latent rather than
live — but any future parser wiring it up would have let a player name what
their own goods sold for. Prices now come from `config.RESOURCE_BASE_PRICE`,
and the field is gone.

Trading skill also *reduced* the sale price, penalising skilled merchants. The
market now quotes a spread around the base price which trading skill narrows in
the trader's favour, without ever inverting it — so buying and selling the same
goods in one place is always a small loss rather than an arbitrage loop.

### Player reports hid most of the turn

`generate_player_reports` rendered a hardcoded list of six phases — movement,
recruit, buy_ship, magic, combat, income. The engine emits around twenty. Every
result from `tax`, `trade`, `sail`, `secure`, `fortify`, `diplomacy`, `assign`,
`collect`, `mine`, `build`, `study`, `teach`, `religion`, `capture` and the rest
was computed, logged, and then dropped before the player saw it — including the
failure reasons that explain why an order did nothing. All phases are now
rendered, in a defined order.

### Turns could be processed twice

`soe process-turn --turn N` never checked `N` against the game's current turn.
Re-running a turn applied its orders a second time to the state they had already
produced. Out-of-sequence turns are now refused unless `--force` is given.

### Smaller items

- `calculate_faction_power` constructed a throwaway `Faction` on every call as a
  `dict.get` default.
- Unit types were compared by `stack.unit_type.name == "SOLDIER"` rather than
  against the enum.
- `Faction.treasury` was typed `int` but held a float after the first upkeep
  deduction; it is now `float`, and reports format it to one decimal.
- `pyproject.toml` declared version `0.1.0-alpha` while the README claimed
  v0.7.0.

## 3. Design debt closed in v0.7.2

The v0.7.1 audit left six limitations as accepted rather than fixed. Four are
now closed, with regression tests in `tests/test_gap_closures.py` that fail
against the v0.7.1 code. The remaining two are features, not cleanup, and are
scheduled in the README roadmap.

### One fortification store

Fortification level lived in three places at once — `City.fortification_level`,
`Faction.fortifications` and `GameState.city_fortifications` — and combat read
only the last, so a level set through one path was invisible through another.

The city now owns it, which is also where it belongs: defenses stay with the
walls when a city changes hands, rather than following the faction that built
them. The other two fields are gone, and saves written against them are
migrated on load (`storage._migrate`), taking the highest level any of the three
claimed.

### Casualties scale with the margin of victory

Rates were flat — 10% winner, 30% loser — so a 10:1 rout cost the winner exactly
what a coin-flip battle did, and the loser lost no more.

Both rates now scale with the margin between the two combat rolls, clamped at
10:1. At parity they are exactly the old constants, so an even fight is
unchanged; a rout costs the winner as little as 1% and the loser as much as 95%.
Using the rolls rather than the raw powers means an upset — a weaker force that
wins on a lucky roll — is still correctly expensive.

### The leader is an explicit flag

`Character.is_leader` replaces "whichever character iterates first". That choice
decides who draws no salary and who receives orders naming no actor, so it was
silently reassigned whenever a faction's character dict changed order. Saves
without the flag keep the character that would have been picked before.

### Diplomatic stance affects combat

Ally/enemy/neutral were recorded and reported but changed nothing. Now attacking
an ally is refused at validation with a warning naming them, and a defender's
allies who have fighting strength at the location join the defense: their power
counts toward the defense and they take the same casualty rate. A faction allied
to both combatants stays out rather than fighting itself.

Movement rights and non-combat support still ignore stance.

### `AWAIT` and `REPEAT` are honest

They still do not execute — that needs the cross-turn order queue, which is the
v0.9 roadmap item. But they logged a success event as though they had run.
They now fail validation with "Queued orders are not executed yet (planned for
v0.9)", so the player sees the truth in their report.

## 4. Still open

- **No fog of war**: reports are correctly scoped per faction, but the engine
  has no notion of what a faction can observe. Scheduled for v1.0.

## 5. Closed in v0.8

Per-character gold and the cheap-gap command set (prisoner ops, status/stealth,
GET/TRANSFER/UNLOAD, PAY/BORROW/REPAY, HIRE synonym) shipped in v0.8.0. See
`tests/test_v08.py` and `MECHANICS.md`.
